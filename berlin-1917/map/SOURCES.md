# The 1917 map: layers, sources and licences

Every layer in `map/1917/` is in ETRS89 / UTM 33N (EPSG:25833), metres. The tools that make them are in
`tools/map/`; raw downloads go to `cache/` (not in git). The first slice is Friedrichstadt and Alt-Berlin,
389400–392900 E, 5817300–5820800 N (`tools/map/slice.json`).

## How exact it is

- **Position.** The Straube scan sits within 1.5 m of today's survey on 46 of 49 tiles (median 0.5 m east,
  0.5 m south; two east-edge tiles 2-3 m; the Tiergarten tile, with few buildings to match, 7 m), measured on surviving buildings (`cache/<slice>/align_straube_alkis.json`). Traced polygons
  are moved by a smooth field (`offsetfield.ts`): the 22 tiles measured surely keep their own shift, the rest the median,
  blended bilinearly between tile centres so seams stay whole (largest move 2.1 m from the median).
- **The traced half.** The Straube reader (tools/map/model/straube.py) is scored on three held-out tiles of the 1900
  coverage: built-up IoU 0.84, private 0.80, public 0.80, water 0.90-0.94 after the colour gate (gate.ts), parks
  0.19 (`cache/<slice>/model/log.json`, `out/map/checks.txt`). Railway land (0.24 IoU as read) now follows the
  plan's lilac tint, which takes none of the truth tiles' streets, buildings, water or parks. Private buildings are split
  along the plan's lines (410 pieces for 416 houses on a test tile); slivers under 2 m wide are dropped.
- **Known answers.** All ten landmarks of 1917 present, the Air Ministry of 1936 absent, Vossstrasse's north side
  separate houses (`tools/map/check.sh`).
- **Street or courtyard.** Points every 20 m along OpenHistoricalMap's 1917 street centrelines that fall on open ground
  fall on street 98.0% of the time (`checkstreets.ts`; before 11-17, when pieces were split by size, most courtyards
  were street). Gardens the reader misses (parks 0.19 IoU) are courtyard.
- **Sewers.** Hobrecht's Blatt 4 is fitted on 68 blocks (median 4.0 m off, 90% within 7.2 m), then bent locally onto
  our street edges (shift median 3.5 m). 94% of the sewer's length lies outside buildings, 1.2% more than 3 m inside one.
- **Use.** The 1910 map is generalised and sits 2-13 m off per tile (fitted on our streets); a building takes the
  most common use under it, so small buildings at a block's edge can take the neighbouring block's.
- **Not yet in:** heights other than the two rules, Systems I, II and V of the sewers, and soil. research/map-p0.md lists what is not right yet;
  research/replicate.md is the plan for the rest.
