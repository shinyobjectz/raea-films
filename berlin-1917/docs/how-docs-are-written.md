## What the numbers say

**These films aren't slow because they're quiet.** Narration covers 85–99% of their running time, at 136–200 words a minute, which is faster than our 145. I'd assumed the fix was more silence. The data says it isn't.

**What makes them feel unhurried is how long they stay with one thing.** A section runs 1–5 minutes on one question. Our draft changes subject about every 14 seconds: 34 paragraphs, each landing a fact and moving on. That's the "Next:" feeling.

**Their sentences are longer and joined.** Their median sentence is 11–19 words; ours is 9, the shortest of all, and a quarter of ours are 6 words or fewer. They link sentences with words of cause and time: *because, but, until, so, by the following day, at first … but, they didn't know it then, but*. We stacked one-line facts. Our own style guide caused this, by asking for one idea per sentence and a short beat every few lines.

## How they're built

1. **They open on one person at one moment, told as a scene, then pull back.**
   - Vox: "Sadie Pfeifer was 9 years old when this photo was taken. Operating heavy machinery that's nearly twice her height … She was just one of many children … they all had one thing in common: they all met Lewis Hine."
   - Fall of Civilizations spends two minutes on a ship blown off course and a dead man found in the snow, before saying a word about history.
2. **They set the stakes before the method.** Vox: "State legislatures and the American public knew this was happening on a mass scale, but didn't act. Until they saw what it actually looked like." Only then does the photographer appear.
3. **The sources speak.** Vox brings in Hine with his own words, read as a quote. Fall of Civilizations reads the old account aloud ("On his head was a hood, well-made …"). The record talks in its own voice, not just through the narrator's summary.
4. **The hook is a question the whole film answers, and the film comes back to it.**
   - LEMMiNO asks "What was the puzzle for? Who was behind it? What happens when you reach the end?" and repeats the same three questions at the end of chapter one.
   - Sections end on a turn: "However, as it later turned out, this was only the beginning." "However, this was still not the end."
5. **Sections start on a time or a place, then tell what happened in order.** "At the turn of the twentieth century …", "Starting in 1908 …", "At the beginning of 2014 …", "So now I'm headed back to the studio to figure out what is going on …".
6. **The search is a journey, with confusion and dead ends told in time.** Johnny Harris and Phil Edwards say they don't understand yet ("I'm kind of confounded by this and I need your help"). A dead end is a beat of the story: "At first this appeared to be a dead end, but …".
7. **Short fragments are for montage, not for facts.** Vox's one run of fragments is a list of places over pictures: "Sardine cutters in Maine. Oyster shuckers in Louisiana. Tobacco pickers in Kentucky." Everywhere else, sentences flow.
8. **There are few numbers, and each comes with its meaning attached.** "1.75 million children under the age of 16, more than one in five." The number and what it means are in one sentence.
9. **Imagining is marked as imagining.** "They must have rolled it around in their hands and wondered …" We don't invent, but we can invite the viewer in: "Picture the stair."

## What changes in our script

- **Fewer, longer chapters:** five chapters of 1½–2 minutes instead of nine of under a minute. Each chapter is one question, told in time order.
- **Open on the woman and the room as a scene,** then the fund and why it took the picture. That's our version of Hine: they photographed sick members' homes to show that housing made people ill.
- **The record speaks:** the caption read aloud in translation, and the address book's own lines.
- **Sentences joined by cause and time,** at a median of about 15 words. Short sentences only for turns and montage.
- **The building stop moves to where it explains something:** a 1910 plan can show the 1917 city because the city stopped building in between. It stops being a statistic dropped into the war winter.
- **Every chapter ends on a turn** that the next one picks up. The film's question comes back at the end.
- **About 1,000 words for 8 minutes:** 145 words a minute over 85% of the time after a 50-second cold open.
